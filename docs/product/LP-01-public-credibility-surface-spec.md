# LP-01 — Public Credibility Surface Product Specification

**Product:** Cheffy Bites
**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** 1 September 2026
**Status:** READY FOR IMPLEMENTATION REVIEW
**Scope:** Public bilingual credibility website and thin, non-transactional Kitchen marketplace demonstration
**Primary audience:** Montreal commercial Kitchen operators
**Commercial phase:** Pre-outreach research and pilot preparation; no live marketplace, inventory, bookings, customers, partners, or validated launch cell

## Document control

This specification is the canonical LP-01 product, UX, and public-content handoff. It does not change the product/business requirements in the Cheffy Bites Master Specification, the hypothesis status in MI-01 v1.1, the desktop-research limits in MI-02 v1.1, or the evidence rules in MI-07A v1.0. Accepted ADRs and the existing canonical architecture documents continue to govern architecture and implementation representation.

The source documents are evidence and product-boundary references, not public claims. In particular:

- all 99 MI-01 hypotheses remain `UNTESTED`;
- no Kitchen operator has been contacted and no operator evidence has been collected;
- eight public rental propositions found in desktop research are not live or marketplace-ready supply;
- no Montreal launch cell has been selected;
- the founder's 30 km maximum is not a service radius; and
- the open-marketplace and managed/committed-capacity models remain separate hypotheses.

All public-facing copy uses ordinary sentence-case spelling of “commercial kitchen,” except for the compact phase label `Chef ↔ Kitchen`. Capitalized domain terms such as `Kitchen` remain appropriate in internal product and technical documentation.

## 1. Executive objective

Build the smallest credible public experience that can accompany founder outreach to Montreal commercial Kitchen operators. Within 30 seconds, an operator should understand:

1. Cheffy Bites is a real, founder-led Montreal software startup.
2. Cheffy Bites is building toward a Chef ↔ Kitchen booking workflow, not a food-ordering marketplace in Phase 1.
3. The concept may help operators receive better-structured opportunities from qualified Chefs and food businesses.
4. Operators would keep control over availability, requirements, pricing, terms, and final approval.
5. Cheffy Bites is still validating the model and is not claiming live demand, inventory, bookings, or regulatory readiness.
6. The operator can speak directly with the founder without completing a heavy onboarding process.
7. A clearly fictional demonstration shows the intended future workflow without posing as a live listing.

The site is successful when it creates informed conversations, not when it maximizes sign-ups. A form submission is a lead or research signal only; it is not market validation, operator activation, supply, or a pilot commitment.

### Recommended public posture

> **Match commercial kitchen capacity with qualified food businesses—while operators stay in control.**
>
> Cheffy Bites is building a Montreal platform to help commercial Kitchen operators connect with qualified Chefs and food businesses. Operators would keep control of availability, requirements, pricing, terms, and approvals.

Required stage disclosure beside the first call to action:

> Cheffy Bites is currently in the research and pilot-preparation stage. No live Kitchen inventory or booking is available on this site yet.

### Non-objective

LP-01 does not validate the business model, recruit or onboard supply, collect compliance documents, create a production marketplace, or authorize operator outreach. It creates an accurate credibility surface for a later founder-approved outreach step.

## 2. Audience hierarchy

| Priority | Audience | Immediate question | Required answer | Primary action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P0** | Commercial Kitchen owner, operator, or rental manager in greater Montreal | “Is this credible, relevant, and safe enough to discuss?” | Founder identity, narrow Phase-1 concept, operator control, research status, two distinct collaboration hypotheses | **Talk with the founder** |
| **P1** | Chef or food business already using or actively seeking commercial Kitchen capacity | “What might this help me find, and is anything live?” | Future structured discovery concept; no live inventory; research-stage invitation | **Join early Chef research** |
| **P2** | Referral source, adviser, insurer, lawyer, industry contact, or prospective collaborator | “What exactly is Cheffy building now?” | Phase-1 boundary, truthful validation status, Montreal focus, contact path | **Learn more / contact** |
| **P3** | Search or social visitor | “What is Cheffy Bites?” | One-sentence positioning and clear stage disclosure | Navigate by audience |

The operator experience takes precedence over consumer appeal. Language, visual design, page order, and calls to action should feel useful to a person responsible for commercial operations, risk, scheduling, and approvals.

## 3. Positioning

### 3.1 Final positioning statement

**Short form:**

> Cheffy Bites is building a Montreal platform to help commercial Kitchen operators connect with qualified Chefs and food businesses—while operators keep control of availability, requirements, pricing, terms, and approvals.

**Hero form:**

> **Match commercial kitchen capacity with qualified food businesses—while operators stay in control.**

This improves on “Turn commercial Kitchen capacity into qualified opportunities” by putting the two sides and the operator-control condition directly in the proposition. It describes the intended product function; it does not claim that Cheffy Bites currently produces demand, utilization, bookings, or revenue.

### 3.2 Meaning of “qualified”

“Qualified” must never imply that Cheffy Bites has independently verified a person, business, permit, insurance policy, or regulatory status. On the site, it means **a Chef or food business that provides the information and evidence required for an operator's own review**. Until an actual verification process is established, use “meets the operator's requirements” or “subject to operator review” near any qualification reference.

### 3.3 Phase-1 boundary

Public positioning is limited to helping Chefs and food businesses find and request existing commercial Kitchen capacity. Do not market food ordering, delivery, Dietitian services, multi-Chef consumer orders, subscriptions, ratings, or other later-phase concepts.

### 3.4 Montreal positioning

Use “Montreal” or “greater Montreal” as the research geography. Do not name a launch neighbourhood, show a coverage circle, promise citywide coverage, or use the founder's 30 km maximum as a service radius. If a map motif is used, it must be decorative and must not imply coverage or inventory.

### 3.5 Two separate operator hypotheses

**A. Open marketplace / qualified availability requests**

- Cheffy Bites may help a food business discover a suitable Kitchen and send a structured request.
- The operator retains its Kitchen, calendar, pricing, requirements, contract, and final decision.
- This is being explored; no operator participation is claimed.

**B. Managed / committed-capacity pilot**

- Separately, Cheffy Bites is exploring whether an operator might reserve a private unit, recurring block, off-peak block, or committed production hours for a small pilot.
- Cheffy Bites could schedule only capacity explicitly granted under written, agreed terms and only for users the operator has approved where required.
- A right to rent does not grant a right to re-rent, sublicense, resell access, or schedule third parties.
- This is not a current commercial offering and must never be merged into the open-marketplace card or flow.

## 4. Claims guardrails

### 4.1 Allowed claim patterns

Use language that describes intention, research, or a future workflow:

- “Cheffy Bites is building…”
- “Cheffy Bites is exploring…”
- “The future experience could…”
- “Depending on operator needs…”
- “We are preparing for conversations with Montreal operators…”
- “Operators would retain…”
- “Subject to operator review and agreed terms…”
- “Demonstration only—nothing is available to book.”

### 4.2 Claims requiring immediate qualification

| Term | Required qualification |
|---|---|
| Qualified request | “Based on information required by the operator; subject to operator review.” |
| Availability | “Operator-confirmed” or “example/demo”; never imply a live Cheffy calendar. |
| Pilot | “Research/pilot preparation” unless a real pilot has been expressly agreed. |
| Verification | State exactly who checked what and when; otherwise do not use the word. |
| Compliant / licensed | Attribute to a source and state that each business/activity still requires review; preferably omit from the credibility site. |
| Booking | Use only for the future workflow; the current CTA is “Request availability.” |
| Marketplace | Pair with “building,” “future,” “concept,” or “demonstration.” |

### 4.3 Prohibited claims and devices

Do not publish:

- “Montreal's leading” or “Canada's leading” marketplace;
- user, operator, listing, demand, booking, revenue, or utilization counts;
- “active demand,” “guaranteed bookings,” “guaranteed occupancy,” or “guaranteed revenue”;
- percentage improvement or earnings claims;
- live availability, instant booking, or “book now”;
- a verified network, regulatory approval, full compliance, or product-market fit;
- partner, customer, or operator logos;
- testimonials, reviews, ratings, case studies, or press mentions that do not exist;
- a real operator's name, address, photo, rate, equipment, or terms in the demo;
- a service-area map or 30 km coverage statement;
- “Cheffy Bites Inc.” or any incorporation, office, or business-address statement not confirmed by the founder;
- “pilot underway,” “launching now,” or equivalent language;
- stock counters, fake activity notifications, waitlist totals, or “spots remaining” scarcity;
- legal, insurance, permit, or safety assurances; or
- any suggestion that Cheffy Bites can currently re-rent or schedule third-party access.

### 4.4 Required trust signals

Every page must show the real Cheffy Bites name, Montreal/Quebec location at city/province level, a verified domain contact, an English/Français switch, and a footer link to the privacy notice. The homepage, operator page, Chef page, and demo must show a stage or demo disclosure before a visitor could mistake the site for a live marketplace.

### 5.1 Language-switching UX

- The header switch is a normal text link labelled `FR` on English pages and `EN` on French pages, with an accessible name such as “Voir cette page en français.”
- Switching languages keeps the visitor on the equivalent route.
- Each localized page has its own stable URL, correct document `lang`, canonical URL, and reciprocal `hreflang` links.
- Do not auto-redirect based on browser language. A visitor can select a language explicitly; remembering that selection is optional.
- A missing translation must not silently fall back to mixed-language content. Block publication of that route until both versions are approved.

## 6. Homepage wireframe and content

### 6.1 Page objective

Establish seriousness and explain the operator-controlled Phase-1 concept in one short scroll without looking like a mature marketplace.

### 6.2 Recommended section order

1. **Header** — Cheffy Bites wordmark, `For Kitchen operators`, `For Chefs`, `Demo`, language switch, and compact `Talk with the founder` button.
2. **Hero** — final value proposition, supporting copy, explicit research-stage note, primary and secondary actions.
3. **What Cheffy Bites is** — one paragraph defining the narrow Chef ↔ Kitchen concept and excluding food ordering from the current phase.
4. **How it could work** — three future-tense steps: share requirements/capacity; receive or send a structured request; operator reviews and decides.
5. **For Kitchen operators / For Chefs** — two concise audience panels, with the operator panel visually first and slightly larger.
6. **Two ways Cheffy is exploring collaboration** — separate, equal cards for open requests and committed capacity, with a rights warning under the managed card.
7. **Why Cheffy is speaking with Montreal operators** — transparent research questions, not a sales pitch.
8. **Demo preview** — one screenshot or interface card carrying a persistent `Demonstration listing` label and a link to the demo.
9. **Founder/company** — a real founder name and concise execution-credibility paragraph, subject to founder confirmation.
10. **Final operator CTA** — `Talk with the founder`, one-sentence low-commitment explanation.
11. **Footer** — location, contact, stage note, privacy, terms, and language links.

### 6.3 Content limits

- Target 650–900 English words and an equivalent natural French version.
- No carousel, logo cloud, testimonial area, pricing table, metric strip, blog feed, countdown, waitlist count, or “featured Kitchens.”
- Use at most one hero image and one demo-interface image. The layout must remain credible if imagery is removed.

### 6.4 Homepage interaction

- Primary CTA: `/kitchens/join`.
- Secondary CTA: `/demo/kitchen`.
- Audience panels link to `/kitchens` and `/chefs`.
- Do not open an unsolicited modal. Calls to action navigate to predictable pages.

## 7. Kitchen operator page

### 7.1 Page objective

Give an operator enough information to agree to a conversation without implying that every operator experiences the same problem or that Cheffy Bites already has demand.

### 7.2 Wireframe

1. **Hero:** “Explore a more structured way to receive relevant Kitchen requests—without giving up control.”
2. **Stage disclosure:** research and pilot preparation; no operator network or live requests.
3. **Friction Cheffy is exploring:** discovery, initial qualification information, availability matching, structured requests, booking workflow, and repeat scheduling. Introduce the list with “Depending on the operator's current workflow…”
4. **Operator-control panel:** availability, pricing, accepted uses, qualification requirements, commercial terms, and final approval remain with the operator.
5. **Two separate collaboration cards:**
   - `A — Receive structured availability requests`
   - `B — Discuss a reserved or committed-capacity pilot`
6. **What Cheffy needs to learn:** current rental formats; information needed before review; acceptable Cheffy role; continuing value after an introduction; French workflow needs.
7. **What this is not:** no commitment, no automatic listing, no instant booking, no request for permits/contracts/documents through the site.
8. **Primary CTA:** `Talk with the founder`.
9. **Secondary CTA:** `See the demonstration listing`.

### 7.3 Operator-control statement

The following statement must appear above both collaboration cards:

> **Your Kitchen, your decision.** A Cheffy request would not create a booking. You would decide what capacity to discuss, what information a food business must provide, what price and terms apply, and whether to approve any request.

### 7.4 Managed-model warning

Place this directly inside the managed-capacity card:

> Cheffy Bites does not currently hold Kitchen capacity or third-party scheduling rights. Any pilot would require explicit operator permission and appropriate written terms. Renting capacity alone does not grant a right to re-rent, sublicense, resell, or schedule access for others.

### 7.5 Page acceptance checks

- The two models remain understandable if their card headings are read without body copy.
- No sentence assumes unused capacity, pain, demand, or willingness.
- “Qualified” is tied to operator-defined requirements and final review.
- The first CTA is visible without scrolling on common laptop and mobile viewports.
- The operator can reach the form in one action and the founder/company details in two actions or fewer.

### 8.2 Wireframe

2. **Stage disclosure:** no live Kitchens, prices, or booking calendar.

6. **CTA:** `Join early Chef research`.

### 8.3 CTA behavior

Minimum fields are business email, preferred language, and an optional one-sentence Kitchen need. Name is optional. If the privacy/form service is not ready, use a founder-approved domain email link and track only the click; do not create a third-party waitlist with undisclosed processing.

Do not call Chef responses “demand,” “active users,” or MI-06 evidence. Any later discovery work must follow its own approved research instrument.

## 9. Operator pilot/contact form

### 9.1 Form purpose

Start a founder conversation and categorize broad interest. It is not operator onboarding, listing creation, compliance screening, a pilot agreement, or proof of supply.

### 9.2 Field specification

| Field | Control | Required? | Rationale and data-minimization decision |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Business / operator name | Text, 2–120 characters | Yes | Establishes business context; do not ask for a legal entity number. |
| Contact name | Text, 2–100 characters | Yes | Allows a personal response; disclose that this is personal information. |
| Role | Short select plus “Other” | Yes | Helps find the appropriate person without inferring authority. Options: Owner/founder, Operations, Kitchen rentals, Property/facility management, Other. |
| Business email | Email | Yes | Primary reply channel. Reject obvious invalid format; do not require a consumer email if a business email is unavailable. |
| Phone | Tel | No | Useful only when the contact prefers a call. Never require it. |
| Facility area | Text or short select | No | Ask only for borough/municipality or general area, not a street address. |
| Kitchen type | Multi-select | No | Shared/commissary, production, ghost/delivery, private unit, restaurant/caterer capacity, other. Do not force classification. |
| Current rental format | Multi-select | No | Hourly/block, daily, recurring hours, monthly package, private unit/lease, not currently renting, other. A selection is not evidence of availability. |
| What are you interested in? | Multi-select | Yes | Options: speaking with the founder; discussing structured availability requests; discussing recurring/committed capacity; learning more; other. No option is preselected. |
| Preferred language | Radio | Yes | English, français, no preference. This is operational routing, not a promise of end-to-end bilingual service. |
| Message | Textarea, maximum 1,000 characters | No | Allows context; warn users not to include sensitive or confidential information. |
| Collection acknowledgement | Checkbox | Yes | Confirms the person has read the short collection notice. It must not be bundled with marketing consent. |
| Product/research updates | Checkbox | No | Optional, unchecked, separately worded CASL-oriented opt-in. Omit entirely until the sender identity, consent record, and unsubscribe process are ready. |

### 9.3 Do not collect

Do not request or accept uploads of permits, licences, insurance certificates, contracts, leases, tax records, identity documents, employee information, renter/customer names, precise availability calendars, pricing sheets marked confidential, or incident information. The message helper text must say: “Please do not include confidential documents or sensitive personal information.”

### 9.4 Submit behavior

- Submit label: `Request a conversation` / `Demander une discussion`.
- Disable only while a submission is being processed and keep the label understandable.
- On success, show an in-page confirmation and send a simple confirmation email only after sender configuration and privacy review.
- Success copy must not say “You're in,” “pilot accepted,” “listing created,” or “we'll send bookings.”
- If `français` is selected, the confirmation must explain that French-language support can be arranged for the conversation. Do not imply that a bilingual website proves end-to-end bilingual operations.
- On failure, preserve non-sensitive field entries in the page, move focus to an error summary, identify specific fields, and provide the verified public email as a fallback.
- Apply server-side validation, rate limiting, and a privacy-respecting anti-spam measure. Avoid invasive tracking CAPTCHA where a honeypot and rate controls are sufficient.
- Never include form contents in analytics, URLs, client logs, error-reporting breadcrumbs, or marketing tools.

### 9.5 Canonical short collection notice

> Cheffy Bites will use the information you provide to respond to your request and manage related follow-up. We do not ask for confidential Kitchen, permit, insurance, or contract documents through this form. Read the Privacy Policy to learn about access, correction, withdrawal of consent, retention, service providers, and how to contact the person responsible for privacy.

The final notice must name the responsible enterprise/person, relevant third-party categories, and possible processing outside Quebec after the founder confirms those facts.

## 10. Demonstration Kitchen listing

### 10.1 Demonstration identity

**Name:** Atelier Démo Montréal / Montreal Demo Production Kitchen
**Persistent label:** `DEMONSTRATION LISTING — NOT AVAILABLE FOR BOOKING`
**French label:** `FICHE DE DÉMONSTRATION — AUCUNE RÉSERVATION POSSIBLE`

This name, all attributes, all visuals, and all values are fictional. They must not resemble or be derived from a real operator's identity, address, photographs, exact pricing, or proprietary terms.

### 10.2 Required disclosure pattern

Show the demonstration label:

1. above the page title;
2. as a persistent badge on the main listing card;
3. beside any price or availability field;
4. inside the request panel; and
5. in the simulated completion message.

The disclosure must use text, icon, and high-contrast styling—not colour alone.

### 10.3 Fictional listing content

| Attribute | Demonstration value |
|---|---|
| General area | North-central Montreal — fictional demonstration area; no street address |
| Kitchen type | Shared commercial production Kitchen — demonstration |
| Rental formats | One-time four-hour block or recurring weekly block — demonstration |
| Availability handling | Request-only; a real operator would confirm a compatible slot and retain final approval |
| Example schedule | Tuesday and Thursday, 8:00 a.m.–4:00 p.m. — fictional display data, not live availability |
| Included equipment | Six-burner range, convection oven, 20-quart mixer, stainless preparation tables, three-compartment sink — fictional display data |
| Storage | One dry shelf and one refrigerated shelf by prior agreement — fictional display data |
| Access | Scheduled access after operator orientation; loading-area access by arrangement — fictional display data |
| Example uses considered | Meal preparation, catering preparation, and small-batch baking, subject to operator review and applicable requirements — fictional examples |
| Example information requested | Business identity, intended food activity, date/time, equipment/storage needs, and readiness to provide operator-required evidence later |
| Illustrative price | CAD $36/hour; four-hour minimum — fictional UI demonstration only, not a quote or Montreal benchmark; taxes, cleaning, deposits, and other terms would require a real operator quote |
| Decision | Operator approval required; a request is not a booking |

Every value rendered in the interface should carry either a shared `Demo data` badge or be enclosed by a panel whose heading and accessible description clearly state that all values in it are fictional.

### 10.4 Demo request flow

The `Request availability (demo)` action opens a structured, non-persistent request preview with:

- requested date;
- start and end time;
- one-time or recurring pattern;
- intended food/business activity;
- required equipment;
- storage need;
- setup and cleanup time;
- general qualification-readiness checkboxes; and
- optional notes with a warning not to enter real personal or confidential data.

The demo must not request a name, email, phone number, real business name, exact address, or document upload. Submission is simulated on the client and displays:

> **Demo complete. Nothing was sent or stored.** In a future workflow, the operator would review the request, ask for any required information, and approve, decline, or propose an alternative. This demonstration does not represent live Kitchen availability.

### 10.5 Demo image rules

Use original/generated neutral commercial-Kitchen imagery or an interface illustration. Never imply the image is a Cheffy Bites facility or a participating operator. Apply a visible `Demonstration image` caption. Do not use a real operator's exterior, logo, staff, or listing photos.

## 15. Privacy and trust requirements

This section is a product launch checklist, not legal advice. The founder must confirm the exact Quebec and Canadian obligations for the chosen operating identity, form processor, email service, analytics, hosting, and communication practices before publication.

### 15.1 Minimum privacy launch gates

1. **Confirm the responsible identity.** Identify the person or enterprise operating the site. Do not imply incorporation. The person with the highest authority is the default person responsible for protection of personal information unless the function is delegated in writing.
2. **Publish the privacy-responsible contact.** Publish the title and business contact details of the person responsible for privacy on the site and in the privacy policy; do not use a home address.
3. **Complete a proportionate privacy impact assessment.** Review the electronic service before launch, including the form, hosting, email, analytics, logging, support, retention, deletion, access controls, and vendors.
4. **Publish a clear bilingual privacy policy.** It must be accessible from every page and adjacent to each collection point.
5. **Provide a collection notice at the form.** State the purposes, collection method, access and correction rights, withdrawal of consent, necessary third parties/categories, possible processing outside Quebec, and how to reach the responsible person. Keep the notice in plain language.
6. **Collect only what is necessary.** Keep phone, facility area, Kitchen type, rental format, and free-text context optional. Do not collect exact addresses or compliance documents.
7. **Set and document retention.** Define a short lead/research retention period, deletion trigger, backup treatment, and method for honoring access, correction, and consent-withdrawal requests. Do not use “keep as long as needed” without an internal schedule.
8. **Protect the data.** Require HTTPS, encryption in transit, restricted access, strong authentication for administrative access, secure secrets, validation, rate limiting, patched services, backups appropriate to the data, and a confidentiality-incident response/register process.
9. **Map service providers and transfers.** Confirm where form, email, hosting, analytics, error monitoring, and backups process or store data. Assess and disclose outside-Quebec processing where applicable and put appropriate contractual controls in place.
10. **Keep analytics minimal.** Do not use advertising pixels, cross-site tracking, fingerprinting, session replay, or form-field capture. Confirm whether the selected analytics/cookie design needs prior consent or an opt-in control; no non-essential identification, location, or profiling feature should be enabled by default.
11. **Separate response from marketing.** The required acknowledgement covers responding to the inquiry. Product/research marketing updates need a separate, optional, unchecked opt-in, a consent record, sender identification, and a working unsubscribe process if the messages are commercial electronic messages.
12. **Review French legal language.** A qualified Quebec French reviewer should review the privacy policy, collection notice, website terms, consent language, and operational response templates.

Quebec's private-sector privacy law requires, among other things, publication of the privacy-responsible person's title/contact, proportionate privacy governance practices, a privacy policy for technological collection, information at collection, appropriate safeguards, and a privacy impact assessment for an electronic-service project involving personal information. Confirm application and implementation against the current [Commission d’accès à l’information guidance](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/responsable-protection-renseignements-personnels-entreprise) and [Loi sur la protection des renseignements personnels dans le secteur privé](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/P-39.1%20), with professional advice where needed.

If Cheffy sends commercial electronic messages, confirm consent, sender-identification, record-keeping, and unsubscribe requirements using current [CRTC CASL guidance](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm/) and qualified advice. A reply to a person's specific request should not silently add that person to a marketing list.

## 19. Responsive and accessibility acceptance criteria

Target WCAG 2.2 AA direction and verify with automated checks plus manual keyboard, screen-reader spot checks, zoom, contrast, and responsive review.

### 19.1 Responsive behavior

| Context | Acceptance criteria |
|---|---|
| Desktop, 1,024–1,440+ px | Hero may use two columns; text measure remains approximately 60–75 characters; cards use at most three columns; maximum content width about 1,200 px. |
| Tablet, 768–1,023 px | Hero and two-model cards adapt without compressed text; navigation remains usable by touch and keyboard; forms remain one or two columns only where reading order is unambiguous. |
| Mobile, 320–767 px | Single-column reading order; no horizontal scrolling at 320 px; CTAs are full-width where helpful; labels, warnings, and demo status appear before controls; no content depends on hover. |

Test at minimum 320, 375, 768, 1,024, and 1,440 CSS pixels, portrait and landscape where relevant, and at 200% browser zoom.

### 19.2 Semantic and keyboard criteria

- Use one descriptive `h1` per page, logical heading hierarchy, and semantic `header`, `nav`, `main`, `section`, `form`, and `footer` landmarks.
- Provide a visible-on-focus skip link to main content.
- All controls and links are reachable and operable in a logical keyboard order without a keyboard trap.
- Focus indicators are highly visible and are not hidden behind sticky elements.
- The mobile menu, language switch, demo request panel, and validation errors work by keyboard and assistive technology.
- Use native controls when possible; do not make generic containers behave like buttons.

### 19.3 Visual and content criteria

- Normal text contrast is at least 4.5:1; large text and meaningful UI graphics meet at least 3:1.
- Do not rely on colour alone for status, errors, required fields, or demo labels.
- Text can resize to 200% without loss of content or function.
- Touch targets meet the WCAG 2.2 target-size direction; aim for at least 44 × 44 CSS pixels for primary controls.
- Motion is minimal and respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- All meaningful images have useful localized alt text. Decorative imagery uses empty alt text.
- Generated/demo imagery is captioned as a demonstration and is not described as a real facility.

### 19.4 Form criteria

- Every input has a persistent visible label; placeholders are examples, not labels.
- Required/optional status is communicated in text.
- Instructions and error messages are connected programmatically to fields.
- On failed submission, move focus to an error summary with links to invalid fields; retain non-sensitive entries.
- On success, announce the confirmation through an appropriate live region and move focus to its heading.
- Autocomplete tokens are used appropriately for name, organization, email, and telephone.
- The consent acknowledgement and optional updates opt-in are separate controls with distinct wording.

### 19.5 Language criteria

- English pages declare `en-CA`; French pages declare `fr-CA`.
- The language switch names the destination language and equivalent page.
- Screen-reader names, validation messages, email confirmations, metadata, and alt text are localized.
- Do not mix English field labels into French forms or leave an untranslated error path.

## Appendix B — Design direction

### Visual character

Professional, calm, operational, and food-aware. The site should feel like a trustworthy B2B workflow product rooted in Montreal—not a restaurant brand, consumer delivery app, or speculative AI startup.

### Typography

- Use one highly legible open-source variable sans serif such as Inter, with system-sans fallbacks, unless an existing approved Cheffy Bites type system already exists.
- Use confident but not oversized headings: approximately 52–64 px desktop and 36–44 px mobile for the homepage `h1`, adjusted for French length.
- Body text should render at approximately 17–18 px with generous line height and a 60–75-character measure.
- Avoid script, novelty, cartoon, or luxury editorial typefaces.

### Colour

- Deep evergreen or charcoal as the primary trust colour.
- Warm off-white background rather than pure white across every surface.
- Restrained terracotta or copper accent associated with warmth and production, not urgency.
- Neutral slate for secondary information.
- Define accessible hover, active, focus, error, success, warning, and demo states; verify actual combinations against WCAG contrast targets.
- The demo warning must not depend on a yellow/orange colour alone.

### Layout and spacing

- Maximum page width: approximately 1,200 px; prose width: 680–760 px.
- Horizontal gutters: 20–24 px mobile, 32–48 px tablet/desktop.
- Use an 8 px spacing rhythm with generous 72–112 px desktop section spacing and 48–72 px mobile spacing.
- Prefer clear alignment and whitespace over decorative dividers or card grids.

### Components

- Medium-radius cards and controls, approximately 10–14 px; avoid pill-shaped everything.
- Solid primary buttons, quieter secondary buttons/links, visible focus rings.
- Cards require real grouping value; do not place every paragraph in a box.
- Two collaboration cards use equal visual weight and explicit A/B labels. The managed model receives a clear warning panel, not a promotional highlight.
- Forms use top-aligned labels, short helper text, clear optional markers, and no multi-step wizard.

### Imagery and icons

- Prefer original or properly licensed documentary-style commercial Kitchen details: stainless work surfaces, production equipment, storage, scheduling notes, or an operator reviewing a workflow.
- Do not show bustling staff or multiple “members” in a way that implies Cheffy users, supply, or activity.
- Generated images are acceptable only when they are not presented as real Cheffy facilities, operators, or customers. Demo imagery must be labelled.
- Avoid glossy luxury-food plates, restaurant dining rooms, home kitchens, cartoon chefs, toque mascots, and generic handshakes.
- Use a consistent, simple outline icon style. Icons support headings and status; they never replace labels.

### Performance direction

- Use responsive image sizes and modern formats.
- Avoid autoplay video, parallax, large animation libraries, and decorative WebGL.
- The page must remain complete and credible before custom fonts or images finish loading.
