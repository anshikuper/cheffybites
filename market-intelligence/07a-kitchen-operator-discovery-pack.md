## 1. Purpose and scope

This pack enables the founder to conduct consistent 15–25 minute learning conversations with real Kitchen operators and capture evidence about:

- genuinely unused, bookable capacity;
- formats, all-in cost, access, equipment, storage and eligibility;
- approval, rejection, permit, training, insurance and operating workflows;
- willingness to receive structured Cheffy-generated Chef requests;
- willingness to expose or reserve real capacity and participate repeatedly;
- continuing value after an introduction and the risk of direct bypass; and
- the separate managed-supply possibility, including explicit third-party scheduling rights.

Phase 1 is limited to Chefs and food businesses already using or actively seeking rented commercial, shared, commissary, production, ghost or private commercial Kitchen capacity, supplied by existing operators. Food ordering, Restaurants, delivery, Dietitians, consumer demand, MI-03 competitor analysis, MI-06 Chef discovery and implementation architecture are outside this task.

The three locations below are discovery priorities only; no launch cell is selected:

1. North-central Montreal: Crémazie–Chabanel–Saint-Laurent.
2. Montréal-Nord / east Laval.
3. Mile End / Plateau.

The founder's 30 km maximum is a boundary, not evidence of Chef willingness to travel or a service radius. Phase-1 validation cash spend must remain below CAD $2,000. Founder time must be logged and later shadow-priced. French capability is not assumed; translation or escalation may be required. This pack does not recommend any binding Kitchen lease.

### Research questions

1. Which exact units, stations or production areas have capacity in the next 4–8 weeks?
2. Which formats are actually used: hourly, block, daily, weekly, monthly hours, recurring schedule, private unit, lease, seasonal or custom?
3. What is the all-in effective cost for supported representative uses?
4. Which businesses and processes are accepted or rejected, and why?
5. How are permit, training, insurance, sanitation, storage and incident duties handled today?
6. Will operators accept structured Cheffy-generated requests, expose actual capacity, hold slots or facilitate a booking/payment?
7. What would cause an operator to continue using Cheffy after a successful introduction?
8. Can an operator offer a reversible committed-capacity pilot and explicitly allow Cheffy-controlled scheduling of approved independent third-party Chefs?

## 2. Evidence rules

### 2.1 Classification

Classify each material response as exactly one of:

| Class | Use |
|---|---|
| **PRIMARY DOCUMENTED FACT** | Current contract, rate sheet, calendar screenshot, written policy or other operator-controlled document. Record document date and version. |
| **OPERATOR-REPORTED FACT** | The authorized operator describes a current observable process or condition. Not independently verified. |
| **OPERATOR ESTIMATE** | Approximate capacity, renter count, utilization, timing or cost. Capture range, period and basis. |
| **OPERATOR PREFERENCE** | Preferred format, workflow, approval method or commercial structure. |
| **OPERATOR INTENT** | Future willingness, such as “would consider” or “plans to.” Never equate with behavior. |
| **FOUNDER INTERPRETATION** | Analysis by the founder; keep separate from the operator's words. |
| **UNVERIFIED** | Claim without adequate source or authority. |
| **CONFLICTING** | Two sources disagree. Preserve both; do not silently reconcile. |

“We probably would participate” is intent, not acceptance of a real booking. “We have space” is not an exact compatible slot. A public listing is not current vacancy. A booked slot is not a completed usable slot. Right to rent is not right to re-rent, sublicense or resell access.

### 2.2 Source and strength

Record source type as `OPERATOR_DOCUMENT`, `OPERATOR_CALENDAR`, `AUTHORIZED_OPERATOR_INTERVIEW`, `OPERATOR_EMAIL`, `OPERATOR_WEBSITE`, `SECONDARY_DIRECTORY`, `CLASSIFIED`, or `FOUNDER_OBSERVATION`.

Record evidence strength as:

- **STRONG:** current primary document or directly observed operator-controlled record tied to an exact facility/unit/period;
- **MODERATE:** current authorized operator report with specifics but no document;
- **WEAK:** estimate, intent, general statement, directory, classified or source with unresolved authority/currentness; or
- **CONFLICTING:** material sources disagree.

### 2.3 Data-quality controls

- Date every conversation, claim, quote, document and confirmation.
- Attribute every material fact to a source and contact role; do not infer authorization from a job title.
- Use exact quotes only when decision-relevant; never reconstruct a quote from memory.
- Use `UNKNOWN`, `NOT ASKED` or `NOT APPLICABLE`, never a guessed value.
- Preserve conflicts and old-versus-current distinctions.
- Do not collect private home addresses, confidential renter identities or confidential renter documents.
- Record aggregate recent-booking behavior without customer names.
- Operator statements are operational evidence, not legal opinions or legal conclusions.
- Do not label any MI-01 hypothesis `VALIDATED` from interviews alone.
- Log founder preparation, interview, follow-up and data-cleaning minutes for later shadow pricing.

### 3.1 Sample and sequence

Approach five operators in the first wave. Stop and synthesize after five completed conversations or after two documented refusals plus three completed conversations, whichever occurs first. Expand only to resolve an important repeated uncertainty or test a missing format; this is not statistically representative research.

## 9. Core operator interview guide

### Interview flow and timing

Use the bold questions as the common core. Use the probes only where relevant; this is a conversation, not a 50-question survey.

1. Facility and renter context — 3 minutes.
2. Current capacity and recent real bookings — 5 minutes.
3. Approval, pricing, compatibility and workflow — 7 minutes.
4. Current acquisition and open-marketplace concept — 4 minutes.
5. Managed-supply module where relevant — 3 minutes.
6. Closing and follow-up permission — 2 minutes.

### A. Operator and facility context

**“Could you walk me through the Kitchen capacity you operate and how external businesses actually use it today?”**

Probes:

- Number of distinct facilities, private units, stations, islands or production areas; shared versus private.
- Typical renter and common food-business categories.
- How long the operator has rented to external businesses.
- Formats actually used most: hourly, block, daily, weekly, monthly hours, recurring schedule, private unit, lease, seasonal or custom.
- Who has authority to approve renters, quote terms and commit capacity?

### B. Current capacity and vacancy — highest priority

**“Looking at the next four to eight weeks, which exact units or work areas have capacity you genuinely want to fill?”**

For every candidate unit/area, ask:

- Which weekday and exact start/end time?
- One-time or recurring? For how many weeks?
- Minimum duration and setup/cleaning time?
- Approximate unused capacity versus capacity the operator wants to sell?
- Peak, unavailable and intentionally withheld periods?
- Can the operator give one exact example now or later from the current calendar?
- When will that availability expire or need reconfirmation?

Do not ask only “Are you available?” Do not enter a slot in the ledger unless the operator ties it to a unit/station, date or recurrence, time and current terms.

### C. Recent real bookings

**“Thinking about the last four to eight weeks, how have external renters actually used the space?”**

Probes:

- Aggregate number of external renters and approximate repeat share.
- Typical format, duration and daypart.
- Periods when space sat unused and periods when demand was rejected.
- Why renters stop, cancel or fail to complete.
- No customer identities are required.

### D. Application, approval and rejection

**“From a new inquiry to approval, what do you need to see, and what are the most common reasons you reject an applicant?”**

Probe business registration/identity; facility-specific permits; MAPAQ-related requirements; trained manager/food-handler evidence; liability insurance; food categories; allergens; frying; smoking; baking; high-volume production; packaging; catering; meal prep; delivery/ghost use; and references/deposit where relevant.

For each rejection, assign a reason code from section 17 and capture whether the restriction is legal/contractual, insurer-imposed, facility policy, capacity-driven or preference. Do not convert an operator explanation into a legal conclusion.

### E. Pricing and commercial terms

**“Could you explain the complete current price and terms for the formats you actually offer?”**

Capture only applicable items: hourly/block rate; minimum hours; weekly/monthly commitment; private rent; lease length; deposit; utilities; taxes; storage; cleaning; equipment; security/access; setup; overtime; cancellation; rescheduling; damage; late/no-show; renewal and termination.

Then ask for the **all-in effective cost** of each supported representative use: 4 hours, 8 hours, 20 hours/week, 40 hours/week, and monthly/private unit. Mark unsupported scenarios `NOT APPLICABLE`; do not force every operator into one model. Ask whether founder labour, onboarding, mandatory visit or paid training adds time/cost to the renter journey.

### F. Equipment and facility compatibility

**“What can a renter count on being able to use, and what varies by unit, schedule or extra charge?”**

Cover ovens, ranges/burners, hood, fryer, mixer, refrigeration, freezer, cold room, dry storage, dishwashing, prep tables, smallwares, specialty equipment, electrical capacity, loading/receiving, courier access, parking/transit, waste/grease, food storage and packaging/staging.

For every important item record one status: `AVAILABLE_INCLUDED`, `AVAILABLE_EXTRA`, `SHARED`, `PRIVATE`, `UNAVAILABLE`, or `UNKNOWN`. Record quantity, condition, booking conflict and whether the operator will later allow a walkthrough or supervised compatibility check.

### G. Operational workflow

**“Please walk me through what happens from a new Chef inquiry to a completed booking and then a repeat booking.”**

Follow the sequence:

`inquiry → qualification → quote → contract → deposit/payment → booking → access → production → cleaning → checkout → repeat`

At each step capture responsible party, tool, usual elapsed time, failure point and exception path. Tools may include website/form, email, phone, spreadsheet, calendar, booking system, contract platform, invoice/payment method, key/code and inspection/checklist.

### H. Current discovery and acquisition

**“How do suitable new renters find you today, and where is the real bottleneck: lead generation, qualification, capacity fit or something else?”**

Probe website, Google, referrals, social, The Kitchen Door, broker, incubator, classifieds and word of mouth. Ask which capacity is hardest to fill, whether vacancy is a problem, whether more renters are desirable and which leads create wasted work. Do not assume the operator wants more demand.

## 10. Open-marketplace questions

Ask only after the current workflow is understood.

### Neutral concept

**“Cheffy is exploring whether qualified food businesses could discover suitable commercial Kitchen capacity and send structured booking requests through one platform.”**

### Questions

1. **“What is your immediate reaction, including any reason you would be unwilling to participate?”**
2. Would you accept a structured request for a qualified, named food business introduced by Cheffy? What must Cheffy collect first?
3. Would you list facility attributes and format/term information? Which information must remain private or request-only?
4. Would you expose actual available blocks? If yes: live calendar, periodic upload, or request-only confirmation?
5. Would you temporarily hold a compatible slot? For how long and under what deposit/cancellation condition?
6. Must the operator retain final approval for every Chef/business?
7. Which role is acceptable: referral only; qualified request; booking facilitation; payment facilitation; or another operator-defined structure?
8. Who should be merchant and contracting party? Who should issue invoices, hold deposits, process cancellations/refunds and handle disputes?
9. Would you allow a later real, named, qualified Chef-request test? What evidence would you require before deciding?

### Evidence discipline

- Interest without a slot is Level 1 at most.
- An exact slot and terms can reach Level 3.
- Only acceptance of a real qualified request can reach Level 4.
- Do not describe a request as “booked” until operator acceptance and all required conditions are met.

## 11. Managed/master-lease questions

This is a separate, late-stage module. Do not imply that Cheffy already controls demand or can accept lease risk.

### Neutral concept

**“Separately from referrals, I’m testing whether an operator might offer Cheffy a private unit or committed block. Cheffy would control only the agreed booking capacity and schedule independent Chef businesses that you have approved.”**

> **RIGHT TO RENT ≠ RIGHT TO RE-RENT / SUBLICENSE / RESELL ACCESS.**

### A. Format first

**“Would you consider any of these for a small validation pilot: an entire private Kitchen, a dedicated station, fixed recurring hours, reserved weekly blocks, off-peak committed capacity or another short pilot block?”**

For each possible format, capture exact unit/block, dates/hours, minimum term, deposit, all-in cost, access, exclusivity and operator approval process. Do not start with a long-term lease.

### B. Third-party user-rights kill question

**“If Cheffy reserved or rented this capacity, would your agreement permit Cheffy to schedule multiple named and pre-approved independent Chef businesses into that capacity?”**

Clarify in the operator's own terminology: employee, independent business, subtenant, licensee, guest, third-party user or permitted occupant. Ask who approves each business and whether the permission can be written into proposed terms. A casual “probably” is not permission.

### C. Terms

Capture unit/block; duration; minimum term; deposit; base/additional rent; utilities/taxes; cleaning; storage; equipment; maintenance; access; keys/codes; security; hours; exclusivity; renewal; termination; cancellation; damage; incident responsibility; additional insured; permit evidence; and approved-user process.

### D. Pilot flexibility

Test, without disclosing a need to spend the full budget:

- one- or two-week block;
- several recurring hours/week or off-peak block;
- cancellable one-month arrangement;
- non-binding LOI or conditional agreement;
- revenue-share or operator-funded pilot; and
- low/no-deposit test.

Record any cash exposure and keep aggregate Phase-1 validation cash below CAD $2,000. No binding lease is recommended by this pack.

## 12. Regulatory/insurance questions

Seek descriptions and documents about the operator's current practice, not legal advice.

**“How do you handle this today with your existing renters?”**

Ask:

- Who holds the facility permit, and what permit or authorization must each renter/business hold for its exact activity?
- How are food-safety-manager and food-handler training requirements checked and recorded?
- How are multiple businesses at one address handled in current onboarding, records and inspections?
- Who is responsible for sanitation, cleaning verification, allergens, traceability, receiving, separated storage, waste and grease?
- Are there occupancy/use or landlord restrictions affecting external businesses, guests, subtenants or licensees?
- What liability coverage, limits, exclusions and additional-insured wording are required?
- Who bears food/product, property/equipment and third-party injury risk?
- How are incidents, damage, complaints and regulator/insurer notification handled?
- In the open model, who contracts, invoices, takes payment/deposit, cancels/refunds and resolves disputes?
- In the managed model, what changes when Cheffy controls capacity and schedules approved users?

For each relevant item record documentation status: `DOCUMENT_AVAILABLE`, `DOCUMENT_REQUESTED`, `NOT_AVAILABLE`, or `NOT_ASKED`. Do not request confidential renter documents. A later targeted professional review is still required where roles or authoritative requirements remain unresolved.

## 13. French-operability questions

## 14. Continuing-value/disintermediation questions

Ask explicitly:

**“After Cheffy introduces a Chef and you complete one booking successfully, why would either of you continue using Cheffy instead of working directly?”**

Then probe, without assuming value:

- availability/calendar management;
- qualification, insurance or document collection;
- payment, deposits and consolidated invoices;
- reminders, cancellation, rescheduling or replacement;
- keys/access and cleaning verification;
- support, dispute and incident handling;
- utilization reporting or analytics;
- repeat scheduling and reputation/reviews.

Capture the operator's own desired value, acceptable workload and economic condition. Ask which function must persist on every repeat booking, what fee or friction would trigger bypass and whether the operator would contractually restrict direct dealing. Do not recommend a restriction; record the response and distinguish preference from enforceable written terms.
