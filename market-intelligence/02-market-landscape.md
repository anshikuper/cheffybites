# MI-02 — Phase-1 Market Landscape & Montreal Kitchen Supply Map

**Version:** 1.1
**Research cut-off / access date:** 31 August 2026
**Decision geography:** Montreal metropolitan area; candidate outreach areas only
**Validation scope:** Phase 1 — Chef ↔ Kitchen booking only
**Canonical status:** Ready to freeze as a desktop-research baseline; not a marketplace validation or GO decision

> **Phase boundary.** “Phase 1” means the MI-01 validation scope in which a Chef finds and books commercial Kitchen capacity. Food ordering, Restaurants, delivery, Dietitians, and multi-Chef consumer orders are outside MI-02. MI-03, MI-06, and MI-07 have not been started in this report.

## 1. Executive summary

### Answer to the core research question

**Conclusion — FACT plus bounded interpretation:** Montreal has a visible ecosystem of commercial/shared/production Kitchen rental propositions sufficient to justify direct discovery. The reconciled inventory contains **15 de-duplicated records**: **8 current public rental propositions**, **2 additional qualified or conflicted leads**, **3 stale/historical records**, **1 non-commercial/not-relevant program**, and **1 outer-area format lead**. These five buckets are mutually exclusive and sum to 15.

That count is not marketplace supply. It does not establish current vacancy, a bookable slot, Chef eligibility, equipment fit, acceptable dayparts, permission for Cheffy-mediated booking, or successful use. The evidence ladder is:

> **PUBLIC RENTAL PROPOSITION → CURRENT VACANCY → BOOKABLE SLOT → CHEF-COMPATIBLE SLOT → RELIABILITY-ADJUSTED MARKETPLACE SUPPLY**

MI-02 reaches only the first step for eight records. It reaches none of the later steps for any operator.

The visible market is **genuinely mixed**, but the mix is asymmetric. Food Factory and Le Kitch show hourly or commitment-priced shared use; Bocolocal uses monthly minimum-hour packages; Kitchub, Co-Work, and Kitchen Six emphasize private or lease-like units; Club Coco is embedded production capacity; and Cabane Renaud is seasonal. The evidence therefore skews toward **recurring, private-unit, operator-controlled, and commitment-based capacity**, not abundant instant-bookable hourly inventory.

**PRIVATE UNIT AVAILABILITY ≠ SPOT MARKETPLACE LIQUIDITY.** Kitchub’s private Kitchens and Kitchen Six’s named units are each one operator/facility concentration, not many independent suppliers.

### Decision discipline

- No public rental proposition is called verified bookable capacity.
- No launch service cell is selected. North-central Montreal, Montréal-Nord/east Laval, and Mile End/Plateau are **candidate outreach/discovery areas** only.
- The founder’s 30 km figure remains a maximum boundary, not a fixed service radius or evidence of willingness to travel.
- No MI-01 hypothesis is marked `VALIDATED`; all relevant hypotheses retain their MI-01 status.
- The managed/master-lease model is **POSSIBLE BUT HIGH-RISK** and worth a tightly scoped direct test, not a lease commitment.
- The Phase-1 cash validation boundary remains below CAD $2,000. MI-02 does not recommend signing a commercial lease.

## 2. Scope and methodology

### Scope applied

- Target side: Chefs and food businesses that already use or seek commercial/shared/ghost Kitchen capacity.
- Supply side: existing Kitchen operators and current sublet leads; no Cheffy-built facilities.
- Geography: Montreal metropolitan area, with outer-area examples separated from Montreal-core evidence.
- Product action: discovery and booking of Kitchen capacity only.
- Evidence cut-off: public evidence accessible by 31 August 2026.

### Reconciliation method

1. Reopened MI-01 v1.1 for hypothesis meaning, phase, priority, status, and evidence discipline.
2. Treated MI-02 v1.0 as the document being revised and MI-02B as adversarial input, not authority.
3. Rechecked operator-controlled pages first; used official sources for regulatory facts and current secondary sources only for corroboration, conflicts, or anonymous leads.
4. Reconciled every MI-02 operator plus Le Cul de Poule; de-duplicated operators/facilities before counting.
5. Reopened the Food Factory term, Kitchub price, Le Kitch currentness, Maz/Levant continuity, and Auteuil rate disputes.
6. Recalculated mutually exclusive inventory buckets from the final classifications.
7. Separated observed facts from interpretation and direct-verification questions.

### Material limitations

This is desktop research. No operator or Chef was contacted; no calendar was authenticated; no booking, payment, access, inspection, licence check, insurance check, or travel-time experiment was completed. Search coverage is incomplete. Public sites can remain online after operations or terms change. Operator claims such as “MAPAQ compliant,” “C1,” “certified,” or “food-safe” were not independently audited.

## 3. Evidence classification

### Claim classes

| Class | Meaning in MI-02 |
|---|---|
| **FACT** | Directly attributable to a cited source. A fact about an advertisement is not a fact about live inventory or compliance. |
| **ESTIMATE** | A disclosed calculation or synthesis. No market-size estimate is produced because reliable inputs are absent. |
| **ASSUMPTION / BOUNDARY** | A founder or MI-01 constraint, such as the 30 km maximum or the validation budget. |
| **HYPOTHESIS** | A proposition still requiring MI-01-standard testing. |

### Final rental classifications

| Classification | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **CURRENT PUBLIC RENTAL PROPOSITION** | A current-access operator-controlled source explicitly offers external Kitchen rental/use. |
| **CURRENT PUBLIC RENTAL PROPOSITION — QUALIFIED** | The public proposition is current-access, but material terms, address/currentness, format, or eligibility require direct confirmation. |
| **LIKELY / DIRECT VERIFICATION REQUIRED** | Rental evidence is plausible, but identity, authority, continuity, or source strength prevents current-proposition treatment. |
| **STALE / HISTORICAL** | Past activity or a no-longer-available offer; not current supply or pricing. |
| **NOT COMMERCIAL THIRD-PARTY SUPPLY** | The source concerns a different purpose, such as community food programming. |
| **OUTER-AREA FORMAT LEAD** | A credible current format outside the Montreal-first evidence area. |
| **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE** | Public evidence does not support a stronger conclusion. |

### Source and conflict rules

Operator-controlled and government sources outrank directories, property listings, news, and classifieds. A current secondary source may corroborate a proposition but cannot prove current vacancy, compliance, or booking completion. Conflicting prices, addresses, names, or operating claims remain **CONFLICTING / DIRECT QUOTE REQUIRED** unless a higher-authority current source resolves them. Multiple units under one operator/facility count once.

## 4. Reconciled Montreal Kitchen landscape

The reconciled landscape contains five commercially distinct patterns:

- **Shared/recurring production:** Food Factory, Le Kitch, and Bocolocal.
- **Private or lease-like units:** Kitchub, Co-Work, and Kitchen Six.
- **Embedded/venue capacity:** Club Coco and the conflicted Maz/Levant lead.
- **Seasonal/special-purpose capacity:** Cabane Renaud.
- **Anonymous sublet capacity:** the H2E classified lead.

The dataset also contains three controls against overstatement: Auteuil is now marked no longer available; Centrale Culinaire’s site is compromised/stale; and Santropol Roulant’s accessible policy is dated 2019. METRO’s program is explicitly community oriented. Le Cul de Poule demonstrates a day-rental/exclusive-use format in Berthierville but does not add Montreal-core supply density.

## 12. Open marketplace model

In the open model, each operator retains control of its Kitchen, calendar, eligibility, contract, and usually access process; Cheffy discovers or facilitates a match. It has lower fixed-cost exposure but weak inventory control. Its early test is whether operators will expose genuine slots and accept a standardized qualified request without the founder manufacturing availability.

The open model remains viable as a hypothesis even if managed supply fails. Its main risks are stale inventory, inconsistent terms, no-result frequency, operator rejection, booking friction, and disintermediation after first contact.

## 13. Managed/master-lease model

**Model:** Kitchen operator → leases a whole/private Kitchen or meaningful committed block to Cheffy → Cheffy controls contracted capacity → Cheffy schedules approved independent third-party Chefs.

This is the **MANAGED / MASTER-LEASE SUPPLY MODEL**. It is distinct from the open marketplace.

> **RIGHT TO RENT A KITCHEN ≠ RIGHT TO RE-RENT / SUBLICENSE / RESELL ACCESS.**

No reviewed public source establishes that Cheffy may rent a private unit or block and resell access to multiple independent Chef businesses. The managed model is therefore classified **POSSIBLE BUT HIGH-RISK**: private and committed formats exist, but re-rental rights, user approval, insurance, permits, occupancy, and budget fit are not public.

The below-CAD-$2,000 validation budget makes a normal commercial lease plus deposit, insurance, and reserve a poor first test. Initial evidence should be an operator interview, written indicative proposal, non-binding LOI, cancellable pilot block, short committed block, or operator-funded arrangement. MI-02 does not recommend signing a lease.

## 15. Master-lease kill test

**Early kill test:** Will at least one suitable operator offer Cheffy either a whole/private Kitchen **or** a meaningful committed block **and** explicitly permit Cheffy, in writing, to schedule approved independent third-party Chefs under agreed contract, permit, insurance, access, cleaning, payment, damage, and incident terms?

If no operator permits this, the managed/master-lease bootstrap model is unsupported in its current form. That result does **not** automatically kill the normal open marketplace model.

## 16. Managed-supply economic framework

The controlling equation is:

> **Minimum effective Kitchen cost per usable hour = monthly fixed Kitchen cost ÷ realistically saleable compatible hours**

Monthly fixed Kitchen cost must include base rent, additional rent/taxes, utilities, insurance, deposit financing/opportunity cost, cleaning, maintenance, equipment, storage, security/access, payment administration, vacancy, refunds, damage/incident reserve, support, founder labour, and desired margin.

Realistically saleable compatible hours must exclude setup, cleaning, maintenance, operator-blocked/event time, access restrictions, seasonal downtime, unsold capacity, and hours that do not match target Chef equipment, food category, duration, or daypart.

| Scenario | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| **LOW UTILIZATION** | Small denominator, substantial unsold capacity, no assumption that nominal access can be resold. Tests downside and cash exposure. |
| **REALISTIC UTILIZATION** | Uses direct evidence on requested/approved/completed hours, downtime, refunds, support, and cleaning. No percentage is invented in MI-02. |
| **HIGH UTILIZATION / CAPACITY-CONFLICT** | Includes rejected demand, changeovers, collisions, maintenance, reserve cost, and service recovery rather than treating all sold hours as frictionless. |

Utilization percentages must be locked before a later experiment/model. Nominal 24/7 access is not the denominator.
